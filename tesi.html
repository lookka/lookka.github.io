Comparing two roads
to classicality.

Overview

This is my Bachelor's thesis in Physics, completed at the University of Turin under the supervision of Prof. Nicolao Fornengo. It compares two families of models used to describe quantum decoherence: collisional decoherence, driven by scattering with an environment of particles, and gravitational decoherence, in which spacetime itself plays the role of the decohering agent.

Undergraduate theses rarely get a second life once discussed — they tend to end up in a drawer, seen by a handful of people. I'm proud of the work I put into this one, so I wanted to give it a proper home here rather than let it disappear.

Abstract

This thesis addresses the long-standing problem of the quantum-to-classical transition through the lens of decoherence theory. After reviewing the formalism of open quantum systems and deriving the general Born–Markov master equation, it develops and compares two mechanisms responsible for suppressing quantum coherence: collisional decoherence, arising from a system's interaction with a thermal bath of environmental particles (modelled following the Caldeira–Leggett approach), and gravitational decoherence, arising from stochastic fluctuations of a Newtonian gravitational field, regularised through a mass-smearing parameter to avoid ultraviolet divergences.

Comparing the two decoherence times as a function of mass, temperature, and spatial superposition scale reveals a critical mass at which the two mechanisms become equally effective. In ordinary conditions — room temperature, atmospheric pressure — environmental decoherence completely dominates and masks any gravitational contribution. Extending the analysis to state-of-the-art optomechanical experiments and, in particular, to the proposed MAQRO space mission — where thermal and collisional noise are suppressed close to their physical limits — shows that gravitational decoherence could instead become the dominant mechanism in a specific mass window, opening a potential experimental route to observing gravitationally induced decoherence.
